# Alex the Locksmith — Interview Batch 1

Date: 2026-02-13
Source: Telegram msg_id 601

## Q1) Origin story (how you started, when/where, why)
- Started at rock bottom; barely knew tools (never used a drill, barely a screwdriver).
- Answered a locksmith ad while job-hunting.
- Discovered a nationwide locksmith scam: foreigners posing as locksmiths, flooding Google Ads/pages.
- Was thrown into the fire; gradually realized it was a scam company.
- Began exiting by making his own locksmith T‑shirts/uniforms (he’s made shirts for years).
- Pivotal incident (Mt Dora, ~2am): rightful homeowner locked out; he drilled and replaced the lock.
- Neighbors called cops; he left, looked up, saw helicopter + multiple police cars, thought “somebody’s in trouble,” then got pulled over.
- Cops were cool after seeing the business card + shirt he made; he was freaked out and realized if they weren’t cool he could’ve been detained.
- Scam company didn’t answer calls; felt left out to dry.
- Quit and started **Winter Park Roadside**, focusing on emergency lockouts; eager to learn.

## Q3) Ideal customer / worst customer
**Ideal customers**
- Family-first men and women who support local business.
- Eager to work with him, willing to spend to solve problems.
- He associates the job with “being Superman for 5 minutes” (24/7 locksmithing).
- Examples: unlocking an EMS truck for a tired paramedic; helping a mother locked out at night with her kid.
- Domestic violence victims; customers with dementia—pride in not taking advantage.

**Worst customers (rare, ~1%)**
- Overly paranoid retired lady who calls him back for “safe issues” that are user error.
- Ego-tripping guy mad for no reason.
- Overall: locksmithing forced him to see how people react in worst situations and made him a better man.

## Q6) Ethics / rules
- Golden Rule: treat customers like you’d treat your own family (mom/grandma).
- Veterans get discounts; sometimes free services.
- Aim for non-destructive entry: **pick first**, avoid drilling when possible.
- Explicitly contrasts with scam training: “we don’t pay you to pick locks… we pay you to drill and charge as much as possible.”

## Q7) Funniest/weirdest call
- Car lockout at a guy’s house: customer was inside playing guitar loud through massive speakers; kept running in and out checking on him while he unlocked the car.

## Q8) Scariest/most tense job
- Early business: owner haggled/refused to pay; he told him he’d put a lien on the building; owner paid (brother was there training).
- Another: customer got mad about price, went to get a bigger “homie” who towered over him; Alex stood firm: “my car isn’t locked—I’ll just leave you with a locked car.” Customer paid.
- Framed as empowering: don’t let customers bully you.
